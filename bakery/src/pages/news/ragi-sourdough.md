# Why we put ragi in our everyday loaf

Ragi, or finger millet, has been eaten in Karnataka for centuries, usually as mudde or dosa. It isn't a usual bread flour, which is exactly why we wanted to try it.

## What it changes

- **Taste:** a gentle, nutty, slightly earthy flavour that goes well with butter and with dal.
- **Colour:** the crumb turns a warm brown.
- **Rise:** ragi has no gluten, so on its own it doesn't make a loaf. We use about one part ragi to four parts wheat. Any more and the bread gets heavy.

## What we learned

Ragi drinks more water than wheat, so the dough needs more hydration and a longer rest. Our first loaves were dry and dense. After a few weeks of small changes, the recipe settled at two days from mixing to baking.

You'll find it on the counter every morning from 6:30 am. If you've tried it, tell us what you think next time you visit.
